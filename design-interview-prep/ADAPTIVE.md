# Adaptive Difficulty Ladder

GMAT-style: strong answers raise the bar for the next question, weak answers ease off — the session converges on the candidate's real level rather than staying fixed.

## The ladder is relative to the selected level

At setup, the candidate (or the person running the practice session) picks one level from one track (see CONTEXT.md — IC: Entry/Senior/Staff/Principal, or Management: Manager/Senior Manager/Director). That selection becomes **Tier 2**, the session's center and starting point. The ladder climbs and falls around it:

- **Tier 0** — two rungs below the selected level. Sanity-check tier, only visited if the candidate is genuinely struggling. One concrete constraint at a time, generous and patient follow-up. Purpose: find the floor, not humiliate.
- **Tier 1** — one rung below the selected level.
- **Tier 2** — the selected level. Every session starts here. Expect the answer to actually clear that level's CDF bar (CONTEXT.md) — a real decision, a number, and a retrospective in every answer.
- **Tier 3** — one rung above the selected level. Follow-ups chain two deep. Constraint injection becomes routine. Less patience for hedging language.
- **Tier 4** — two rungs above the selected level. Near-zero patience for hedging — interrupt and redirect if an answer stalls. Follow-ups chain two to three deep before moving on.

**Clamping at the ends of a ladder.** The IC ladder has 4 rungs (Entry/Senior/Staff/Principal) and the Management ladder has 5 (Associate Manager/Manager/Senior Manager/Director/Senior Director) — a starting level near either end still won't have room for a full 5-tier spread in both directions:

- If the tier calculation would go below the bottom rung of the selected track (e.g. Tier 0 selected as start = Entry or Associate Manager, and the candidate is struggling further), stop dropping — hold at the bottom rung, note in the debrief that the floor was found.
- If the tier calculation would go above the top rung (e.g. start = Principal or Senior Director and the candidate keeps answering Strong), don't invent a fake higher title. Instead keep escalating pressure using **"ceiling+"** framing — ambiguity, constraint count, and follow-up depth continue increasing exactly as Tier 4 describes, and the debrief notes the candidate is "performing beyond the ceiling of the selected level" rather than naming a level that doesn't exist on that ladder.

## What changes between tiers

Not the opening prompt — FORMATS.md openers are already open-ended and scale naturally to any level. What changes is:
- How many constraints get injected at once
- How much ambiguity is left unresolved for the candidate to navigate themselves
- How quickly you interrupt rambling or redirect a stalled answer
- How many layers deep the follow-up chain goes before you move on
- Whether the bar is "does this clear Entry" or "does this clear Principal" per CONTEXT.md's per-level bar summaries

## Per-answer rating (applied after every question + follow-up exchange)

Rate the full exchange — initial answer plus how they handled the follow-up — as one of:
- **Strong** — real decision, quantified outcome, honest retrospective, handled the follow-up without deflecting, and clears the current tier's level bar.
- **Adequate** — got the basics but was thin on one of decision/number/retrospective, or needed prompting to get there.
- **Weak** — descriptive rather than decisive, no real number, deflected the follow-up.
- **Red Flag** — dodged the question entirely, contradicted themselves, or revealed something disqualifying.

## Tier adjustment rule

- Strong → tier **+1** (clamped at the track's top rung, see above)
- Adequate → tier **unchanged**, but escalate the next follow-up one notch harder within the same tier
- Weak → tier **−1** (clamped at the track's bottom rung)
- Red Flag → tier **−1**, and flag this exchange explicitly for the debrief regardless of where the tier lands

## Visibility modes

- **Hidden (default):** never mention the tier, the rating, or the adjustment during the interview. Stay fully in character. The candidate only learns the calibrated level and trajectory in the final debrief.
- **Visible:** after rating and adjusting, say so briefly and plainly before the next question — e.g. "Strong — raising the bar." / "That was thin — let's dial it back a notch." Keep it to one short clause; don't break character to explain the mechanic itself.

## Continuity across the whole session

The tier is never reset when switching formats, and it stays continuous through the required Section 0 (working relationships & tooling fluency, see FORMATS.md) as well as whichever rotating format(s) follow — it's one continuous read of the candidate across the whole session, which is what makes the final "calibrated level reached" meaningful.

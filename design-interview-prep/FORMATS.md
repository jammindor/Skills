# Interview Formats

One required section, then four rotating formats. Each has an opener bank (rotate/pick to fit context) and escalation moves that scale in intensity with the current tier (see ADAPTIVE.md) — the *question* doesn't change much, the *pressure* does. Color every opener and escalation using the company-size/B2B-B2C/competitiveness dials in CONTEXT.md before you say it out loud.

## Section 0 — Required for every candidate

These two run in every session, regardless of which format(s) follow, immediately after setup and before the rotating formats. They're part of the same continuous tier (ADAPTIVE.md) — not a separate warm-up exempt from pushback.

**Working relationship with PM & Engineering**
- Opener: "Describe your working relationship with product management and engineering. What has and hasn't worked in previous roles, and how would you want that relationship to work in this one?"
- Escalations: "Give me the specific moment it broke down, not the general philosophy." / "What do you do differently when the PM is inexperienced versus when they're stronger than you at this?" / "Your engineering counterpart thinks you slow them down — are they wrong?"

**Tool fluency**
- Opener: "Tell me your real experience level with three things, with a specific recent example for each, not just 'I'm familiar': Claude Code or a similar AI coding assistant, Git/GitHub including opening your own PRs, and Storybook or an equivalent front-end component tool."
- Calibrate the bar to the selected level (CONTEXT.md): Entry isn't expected to ship PRs solo; Staff/Principal and Senior Manager+ should have a real, specific story, not just theoretical awareness.
- Escalations: "Walk me through the last PR you actually opened — what was in the diff?" / "Where does Storybook fit in your handoff to engineering today, concretely?" / "If you've never used an AI coding assistant, say so directly — don't perform familiarity you don't have."

## 1. Portfolio review

**Openers**
- "Walk me through the project you're proudest of — start with the business problem, not the UI."
- "Pick the project where you had the least authority but the most impact. What happened?"
- "Tell me about a design you shipped that you'd now call wrong."

**Escalations**
- "What's the actual number this moved? If you don't know, tell me why you never found out."
- "Who on the team thought you were wrong, and why weren't they crazy?"
- "What did you get wrong that data later corrected?"
- "What would your PM say is your biggest blind spot, off the record?"
- "If I called your old manager right now, what's the one thing they'd hesitate to say to my face?"

## 2. Whiteboard design challenge + critique

**Openers**
- "Design an onboarding flow for a B2B tool with a 40% week-one drop-off. Walk me through it out loud."
- "[Name a real, current competitor product's checkout or onboarding flow.] What's broken about it, and what's your fix?"
- "You're designing the 0-to-1 version of [an ambiguous, unnamed product category]. Where do you even start?"
- "Redesign a feature in a mature product that's under pressure to drive monetization, not just usability."

**Escalations (constraint injection mid-answer)**
- "Engineering just told you that's two quarters out. What ships in two weeks instead?"
- "Leadership now wants this to also drive upsell. How does the design change?"
- "Now solve it for a screen-reader user, not just a sighted one."
- "You just lost half your eng headcount for this project. What do you cut first, and why that?"
- "A competitor ships something close to this next month. Does that change your approach at all?"

## 3. Behavioral / leadership

**Openers**
- "Tell me about a real conflict with a peer director over roadmap priority. How did it resolve?"
- "Tell me about a project you shipped that failed. Not a 'failure that was secretly fine' — one that actually failed."
- "Tell me about a time you pushed back on an executive's pet feature."
- "Tell me about upleveling a struggling report on a team that had just been through layoffs."

**Escalations**
- "What did you do differently the next time?"
- "What was the actual cost of being right? Who paid it?"
- "Who still disagrees with you about that decision today?"
- "If I asked that peer director for their version of this story, what would be different about it?"

## 4. Business & metrics acumen

Every question here must resolve into a specific design or interaction decision, not just a business argument — this is a design interview testing generalist range, not a PM/ops/finance panel. If the candidate's answer stays at the level of an argument or an analysis plan, the follow-up should force it back into "so what does the interface/flow actually do differently."

**Openers**
- "This product has three plausible north-star metrics. Pick one, defend it, and tell me the one interaction or design pattern change that would move it most."
- "Finance is asking why design headcount on this project is worth it. Don't argue with slides — show me the one thing in this flow only a designer would catch that an engineer or PM would miss."
- "[Describe a fabricated-but-plausible metrics dashboard: e.g. activation up 8%, retention flat, support tickets up 20%.] What's the one design change you make in the product this week — not the analysis plan, the actual change?"

**Escalations**
- "That metric can be gamed. How, and what would you change in the design — not just the measurement — to make gaming it harder?"
- "You have half the budget you asked for. What's the one design decision you cut first, and why that one over the others?"
- "Translate that into what a CFO actually cares about — then tell me if that changes the actual design decision you just made, or if it's just a better way to explain the same one."
- "Your north-star metric just went up, but a leading indicator you ignored is quietly cratering. Do you notice? What's the specific design change you make in response — not the investigation, the change?"

## Full loop

Section 0 first, then ~2 questions per format, in the order above, carrying one continuous tier across all of it (per ADAPTIVE.md). This is meant to feel like a real onsite loop compressed into one conversation.

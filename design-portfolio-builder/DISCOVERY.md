# Discovery: Intake & Gap-Filling

Phase A's job: turn whatever the user brings — a polished draft, a pile of bullet points, a screenshot of an old deck, or nothing but memory — into a clear map of what's usable, what needs work, and what has to be created from nothing. Do this before writing a single word of new copy.

## Step 1 — Absorb what already exists

If the user pasted or described existing material, read/reread it fully before asking anything. Don't make them re-explain something they already gave you. Identify, for the project in front of you:
- What sections of `FRAMEWORK.md`'s skeleton already have real content (even rough).
- What's implied but not stated (e.g. a decision is described but the alternative they rejected isn't).
- What visuals/artifacts they mention having (screenshots, flows, prototypes) versus what's just described in prose.

If nothing exists yet for this project, say so plainly and move straight to Step 3.

## Step 2 — Triage against AUDIT.md

For each section of the skeleton, classify it as one of:
- **Usable as-is** — real content, right shape. Needs polish/tightening at most.
- **Needs modification** — real content exists but the framing is weak, the lede is buried, it's descriptive instead of decisive, or it's not calibrated to the target level/company from `CONTEXT.md`.
- **Missing entirely** — nothing there. Most commonly: outcome/metrics, the rejected alternative, or the retrospective — these are the three sections people skip when writing from memory.

State this triage back to the user in one short pass before moving on, e.g.: "Your process section is strong as-is. The outcome section needs a real modification — right now it just says 'well received.' And there's no retrospective at all — we'll need to build that from scratch."

## Step 3 — Fill the gaps

For anything modification-needed or missing, ask targeted questions one at a time — don't interrogate with a list. Use these three prompts as the backbone (adapted from `design-interview-prep`'s pushback heuristic, but coaching-toned, not adversarial):

1. **The decision, not the activity.** "What did you actually decide here — and what did you decide *against*? What was the other option on the table?"
2. **The number.** "What's the actual outcome, even approximately? If it was never measured, say so — we'll frame that honestly rather than inventing a number."
3. **The retrospective.** "Looking back, what would you change? If genuinely nothing, push on that — what's the closest thing to a regret or a lesson?"

Other useful discovery questions depending on what's missing:
- "What was the constraint that made this hard? Budget, timeline, headcount, technical debt, a stakeholder who disagreed?"
- "Who else was in the room for this decision, and what was their read on it?"
- "What visuals do you actually have for this — final screens, process artifacts, a prototype link? What's missing that we should note as a to-do outside this session?"
- "AI could generate a flow for this in seconds today — what did you bring that it couldn't?" (Useful for Senior+ case studies where judgment needs to be visible, not just output.)

## Step 4 — Call the biggest gap before drafting

Before moving to Phase B, name the single most consequential gap out loud, calibrated to the target level from `CONTEXT.md` — e.g., a missing number is more damaging for a Senior+ case study than an Entry one; a missing rejected-alternative is most damaging at Staff+ where strategic judgment is the point. If the user can't fill a gap (metric genuinely was never tracked), agree on how to be honest about it in the copy rather than papering over it — see `FRAMEWORK.md`'s guidance on handling unmeasured outcomes.

## Tone

This is coaching, not an interview, in every tone. The goal is pulling a complete, honest story out of the person and their material. The description above is the **Professional** tone (CONTEXT.md's default): warm and specific about what's working, direct and specific about what's missing. If **Brutal** or **Supportive Coach** is selected instead, keep every technique and question in this file exactly as written, and only re-voice the delivery per CONTEXT.md's tone descriptions — Brutal drops the cushioning, Supportive Coach frames gaps as next steps and leans on more earned encouragement.

Whatever the tone, never let a gap slide just to keep momentum or fit the register. A case study that reaches Phase B with an unflagged gap will read as unflagged in the final draft too, regardless of how gently or bluntly that gap was raised.
